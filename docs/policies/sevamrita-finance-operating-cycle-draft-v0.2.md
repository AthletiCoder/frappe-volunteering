# Sevamrita Project and Finance Operating Cycle

Draft for review | Version 0.2 | 9 October 2026

This guide explains how a Center or project moves from an activity plan to an approved budget, receives funds, conducts activities, accounts for spending and reports results. The monthly review then supplies the information for the next month's plan and the next quarterly allocation. Center leaders, project owners, employees, approvers and Accounts each have a part in this cycle.

**The cycle:** Plan activities and budget → approve and create the project → authorise quarterly funds → issue advances or pay vendors → conduct activities → verify and settle expenses → report and reconcile → review and plan again.

## 1 The cycle at a glance

| Stage | Lead person | Record handed to the next stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan the year | Center leader or project owner | Activity plan and costed annual budget |
| Approve and set up | Budget authority and assigned Projects Manager | Approved budget and active project |
| Allocate quarterly funds | Director or designated fund authority with Accounts | Approved allocation and release conditions |
| Request and issue funds | Employee, reporting authority and Accounts | Approved advance and payment reference |
| Deliver and document | Employee and project owner | Activity evidence and bills linked to the project |
| Approve and settle | Receipt reviewer, spending approver and Accounts | Posted expenses, reimbursement or advance adjustment |
| Report and reconcile | Center leader and Accounts | Monthly activity report, financial schedules and exceptions |
| Review and continue | Leadership and project owner | Actions, next month's plan and next allocation decision |

**Keep four amounts distinct.** The annual budget is the approved spending envelope. A quarterly allocation authorises a release of funds. An advance disbursement moves money to a named custodian. A posted expense records an accepted cost. Approving or paying an advance does not, by itself, create expenditure.

**Submission deadline.** The monthly activity report and corresponding financial documents should ordinarily be submitted within **15 calendar days after month-end**. For example, the October pack is due by 15 November. Pending approvals or missing documents must appear in the pack with an owner and follow-up date; they must not delay the whole report.

The Center rules on annual planning, Executive Board approval of reallocations, quarterly releases and supporting evidence are incorporated in this guide. The 15-day deadline and use of the advance **Purpose** field reflect the revised instructions. Additional approval assignments and review dates proposed below are for management review before adoption. The guide complements the accounting policy and approved bill standards.

## 2 Plan the activities and build the budget

**Owner:** Center leader or project owner. **Input:** The Foundation's objectives, prior results, known commitments and proposed activities. **Output:** One activity plan with a costed budget and quarterly funding forecast.

1. List the activities proposed for the year, their purpose, location, expected dates, intended beneficiaries and measurable outputs. Give recurring activities a consistent reference so monthly reports can follow them over time.
2. For each activity, estimate quantities, rates and the expense categories needed. Explain significant assumptions, such as the number of events, participants, journeys or venue days. Identify equipment and other items needing special Accounts treatment.
3. Spread expected activity costs across the four quarters. Forecast when cash will actually be required, including planned supplier payments and employee advances. An even division by four is suitable only when the activity schedule supports it.
4. Identify funding sources and any permitted-use conditions. Record existing unpaid commitments, available project funds and recoverable advances separately. Money already with an employee is not automatically available to fund another activity.
5. Each Center prepares its annual activity and expenditure plan in discussion with **HG Priya Gopesh Pr.** For other programs, use the designated program reviewer. Accounts checks arithmetic, categories, existing balances and whether the proposed funding is feasible.
6. Obtain written budget approval from the designated authority. **Proposed approval assignment:** the Executive Board approves the annual Center budget and its quarterly release plan; management should ratify this assignment. Keep the approved version and approval reference together.

**Before moving on:** The activities, annual total, category totals and quarterly totals agree. The plan names the owner, intended outcomes, funding source and approval reference. Unapproved proposals remain distinguishable from the approved budget.

## 3 Create and activate the project

1. The proposer prepares the project request with its name, purpose, scope, dates, owner, members, cost centre, approved budget and employee-facing expense labels. Attach the activity plan and budget approval.
2. Choose a project structure that can be followed consistently in reports. A recurring Center program may use one continuing project with monthly activity references; a distinct campaign may need its own project. Accounts and program leadership should agree this structure before transactions begin.
3. Assign the **Projects Manager** who will review the request. That manager checks the supporting approval, returns corrections where needed and approves the effective project. Budget approval and project setup approval are separate decisions even when the same person holds both responsibilities.
4. Accounts confirms the cost centre and prepares accounting guidance for the expense labels. The project owner checks that the project is Active, financially open and has the people and labels needed for spending. Basic and Financial members can both claim; their access to financial information differs.

**Ready for funding:** An approved, usable project exists and its budget matches the approved plan. Do not start spending merely because a project proposal has been saved.

## 4 Authorise the quarterly allocation

**Owner:** Center leader or project owner prepares the request; Accounts checks it; the designated fund authority decides the release. **Output:** A dated quarterly allocation record, followed by actual payments when authorised.

1. Review the coming quarter's activities and expected cash payments. Show the approved annual budget, the quarter's planned costs, funds already released, usable money already held and the net additional amount requested.
2. Accounts reviews the preceding allocation against bills, posted expenses, monthly activity reports, cash or bank evidence and outstanding advances. Distinguish a valid remaining float from unsupported expenditure or an unexplained balance.
3. Further allocation is released only after the previous allocation has been adequately supported by the relevant bills and monthly activity reports. Accounts records whether support is complete, what remains open and the recommendation. The fund authority records its decision and any release conditions.
4. **Proposed approval assignment:** the Executive Board or its explicitly delegated fund authority approves the quarterly allocation. Accounts releases money only within that recorded decision, available funds and applicable funding conditions.
5. Enter the approved amount, period, approval reference and actual releases in the allocation register. Individual payments may be staged to match actual requirements. Each advance or supplier payment keeps its own transaction reference.

**Allocation is not expenditure.** An allocation decision does not automatically move money. A payment reference proves a release; a bill and accepted accounting entry prove an expense. Do not replace the annual project budget with the quarterly cash-release amount merely to represent the quarter's funding.

**Funding calculation:** Forecast the cash payments still required for the quarter and subtract funds already held and authorised for those same payments. Include unpaid obligations once. Explain any retained balance, proposed return or restricted money rather than treating every balance as freely available.

## 5 Request and issue an advance

**Owner:** The employee who will account for the money. **Input:** Planned expenditure within an approved activity and funding allocation. **Output:** An approved request, an actual disbursement and a named settlement obligation.

1. Select the intended active project and request the amount actually required. Enter the required-by date and expected settlement date. Use **Purpose** to describe the activity, proposed purchases, amount breakdown and timing. Attach a detailed plan or estimate if it will not fit clearly in the field.
2. Disclose money already held for the same purpose and any related pending request. Choose My expenses or, for an eligible manager holding money for direct reports, Team expenses. Use the approved employee bank details for bank disbursement.
3. The immediate reporting manager reviews necessity, budget alignment, prior utilisation and the employee's live advance exposure. The request proceeds through the reporting chain when higher monetary authority is required. No one approves their own request.
4. Accounts checks the approved request, quarterly release authority, previous-allocation support and actual amount still payable. It arranges the authorised transfer and records the Payment Entry, date and bank reference against the advance. A payment entry alone is not confirmation that the bank transfer succeeded.
5. The employee confirms receipt and keeps the advance reference with the activity and bills. Any change in the activity or expected settlement date is reported promptly to the project owner and Accounts.

## 6 Conduct the activity and submit the expenses

**Owner:** Employee, supported by the project owner. **Output:** Bills and activity evidence linked to the correct project, funding source and advance where applicable.

1. Conduct the approved activity and keep a brief record of what happened, when, where and for whom. Collect the required attendance, delivery, event or other evidence appropriate to that activity.
2. Obtain proper bills or receipts when buying goods or services. Check the supplier, invoice number and date, item details, amount and any applicable tax information against the approved **Sevamrita Foundation Bill standards**. Keep payment evidence as well as the bill.
3. Record the activity reference on the supporting schedule or claim description. Explain how the expense relates to the approved activity. Do not describe an unrelated purchase as part of the project to obtain funding.
4. Submit expenses promptly as bills become available. Select the actual project and expense label, enter the invoice details and attach legible evidence. Use the bill date accurately; submitting in a later month does not change when the purchase occurred.
5. Choose the actual funding source: the employee's own advance, an eligible manager's team advance, or out-of-pocket spending. Link the relevant advance. Where personal money was used, disclose any advance already held so Accounts can prevent duplicate settlement.
6. Retain a claim index linking each bill to its claim, activity, advance and eventual voucher. One invoice creates one accounting claim. A combined submission of several invoices keeps the combined amount for approval authority.

**Supplier route:** Where direct vendor payment is appropriate, use the authorised purchase order, supplier invoice and payment process. Accounts maintains the supplier liability and any supplier advance adjustment. The employee must not also claim reimbursement for that same bill.

**Before moving on:** The bill, activity, project, amount and funding source agree. Missing bills, cancelled activities, cost changes or unapproved purchases are disclosed and referred for correction or an exception decision.

## 7 Verify and approve the claim

1. The **Receipt Reviewer** checks the bill and supporting evidence independently of the claimant. A returned claim goes back for correction and then through receipt review again. Replacing a reviewed attachment requires renewed verification.
2. The **reporting authority** reviews the activity's purpose, eligibility, reasonableness and budget availability, and sanctions the accepted amount. A reviewer with insufficient authority escalates through the reporting chain. Receipt verification is not spending approval.
3. The **Accounts Manager** checks the accepted amount, financial period, funding source and final ledger classification, then finalises the accounts. Manager approval alone does not complete accounting posting.
4. The claimant and project owner follow pending queues until corrections, approvals and Accounts finalisation are complete. Disputed or rejected amounts remain identified rather than disappearing from the utilisation schedule.

**Reallocation rule:** Any transfer between approved budget heads or activities requires **Executive Board sanction before the reallocation is made**. Attach that sanction to the project change request so the assigned Projects Manager can record the approved revision. A portal budget override is not a substitute for this sanction.

## 8 Settle the advance or reimbursement

**Owner:** Accounts performs the settlement; the employee confirms the money held or received. **Output:** A reconciled advance or reimbursement balance with linked vouchers.

1. After Accounts finalises an advance-funded claim, apply the accepted expense to the correct advance. Check the amount actually adjusted, particularly when a bill exceeds the remaining advance or only part of the claim is sanctioned.
2. For an accepted out-of-pocket amount, Accounts checks the outstanding reimbursement and approved bank destination, arranges payment and records the linked voucher and bank reference. The later payment clears the payable; it does not record the expense again.
3. Return unused advance funds through the authorised method. Give Accounts the advance reference and proof. Accounts records and verifies the return against that advance, and the employee confirms the revised balance.
4. If money remains for a future approved activity, document why it is retained, who holds it and when it will be used or settled. Seek the required extension or retention decision. A new advance request does not close an older advance.
5. Accounts resolves unsupported or rejected amounts with the employee and reporting authority. They remain recoverable or under documented review until properly settled; they must not be treated as accepted expenses merely to clear the advance.
6. Close the advance only after posted adjustments and actual returns reconcile. If an approved request still has an unpaid portion that is no longer needed, Accounts resolves that remaining commitment through the appropriate authorised document process.

**Advance reconciliation:** Opening unsettled balance + actual advance payments − posted expense adjustments − recorded and received returns = closing unsettled balance.

**Pending bills:** A submitted but unposted claim appears as pending support. It must not also be deducted as a posted advance adjustment. Show it separately so reviewers understand the expected settlement without understating the current balance.

**Team advance:** The manager remains responsible for the float issued in their name. Maintain evidence of amounts handed to or paid for team members. Accounts matches each eligible team claim to the manager's advance once. A claim marked reimbursed through the manager's float does not prove that a new Foundation bank transfer occurred; confirm the actual handover or payment.

**Completion check:** The claim shows the correct settlement, the advance register agrees with employee balances and accounting records, and the payment or return can be traced to the bank statement or cash evidence.

## 9 Keep the activity and finance records together

Use one controlled project folder or index containing the approved plan, budget versions, allocation decisions, advance references, bill and claim index, payment or return evidence, monthly reports and exception decisions. Use the same project and activity references throughout.

Employees maintain the supporting record as the activity progresses. The project owner checks completeness. Accounts maintains the posted financial schedules and reconciliations. Store sensitive bank details and private evidence only in authorised locations.

## 10 Prepare the monthly report and financial pack

**Owner:** Center leader or project owner consolidates the pack with employees and Accounts. **Deadline:** Ordinarily within **15 calendar days after month-end**. **Output:** A dated report with activity results, financial support and a clear list of open items.

1. Close the activity list for the month. Compare planned activities with those completed, postponed or cancelled. Record actual outputs, participants or beneficiaries and explain significant differences.
2. Gather the month's bills and update the claim index. Identify which claims are posted, awaiting receipt review, awaiting spending approval, awaiting Accounts finalisation or awaiting payment. Also disclose known costs for which a bill or claim is still missing.
3. Accounts supplies the financial schedules: posted expenses by activity and budget head, outstanding vendor and employee amounts, advances, returns and cash or bank balances. Separate the month's costs from payments settling a previous month's costs.
4. Compare expenditure and commitments with the approved annual budget and the quarter's plan. Explain variances, unused allocations and proposed changes. The activity narrative should support and explain the expenditure as far as possible.
5. Reconcile each advance and identify its custodian, closing balance, pending bills and expected settlement date. Include payment and return references. Account separately for genuine Center cash or bank funds and money held by employees.
6. The project owner reviews the pack for completeness and submits it to the Director or designated program reviewer and Accounts through the approved reporting channel. Retain the submitted version and its date. Use the approved **SF Type B Project Monthly Report** where applicable, with the financial schedules below as supporting attachments.

**Do not wait for every claim to finish.** Submit the monthly pack on time, disclose the open items and issue a dated correction or updated financial schedule when those items are resolved. Preserve the earlier submitted version.

## 11 Reconcile and review the month

Accounts compares the pack with the General Ledger, claim and advance records, supplier balances and bank or cash evidence. It checks for duplicate bills, unexplained cash, unsupported advances, missing activity links and expenses posted to the wrong project or period.

Budget Health shows pending commitments as well as approved claims. Accounts reconciles it with posted expenditure and adds supplier or other accounting items needed for a complete project view. Do not add an invoice to a purchase-order commitment a second time for the same spending.

**Proposed review timetable:** Center submission by the 15th; Accounts review and consolidated finance pack by the 20th; leadership review and next-month actions by the 25th. These latter review dates should be confirmed before rollout.

The Director or designated reviewer discusses results, variances and open items with the project owner. Record the decision, responsible person and due date for each action. Accounts retains a month-close checklist and approved schedules; technical fiscal-year settings alone are not the monthly review record.

## 12 Continue the cycle

**Monthly continuation:** Use the review to update the next month's activities, expected cash needs and settlement actions. Carry forward the closing balances and unresolved items without resetting them. Complete any required budget revision before spending under a changed plan.

**Quarterly continuation:** Prepare a utilisation summary for the previous allocation, supported by its bills and monthly reports. Show amounts paid, posted expenses, actual returns, money still held and open support. Accounts reviews the support before recommending the next allocation. The fund authority records the release decision and conditions.

**Annual continuation:** Compare outcomes and costs with the annual plan. Resolve advances, unpaid liabilities, cash balances and funding conditions with Accounts. Use those results to cost the next annual plan and repeat the planning discussion. Completion of a project does not remove the need to settle its remaining liabilities or advances.

## 13 Calendar and handovers

| When | Responsible person | Handover or decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the year's activities | Center leader and program reviewer | Costed plan and annual budget for approval |
| Before a new project starts | Proposer and assigned Projects Manager | Approved project, people and budget configuration |
| Before each quarterly release | Project owner, Accounts and fund authority | Previous utilisation support and next cash requirement |
| Before planned spending | Employee and reporting authority | Advance Purpose, amount and settlement plan |
| During and after each activity | Employee and project owner | Bills, activity evidence and submitted claims |
| Ordinarily within 15 days after month-end | Center leader with Accounts | Monthly activity report and financial documentation |
| By the 20th and 25th respectively, proposed | Accounts and leadership | Reconciled pack, review decisions and next plan |
| Before changing budget heads or activities | Project owner and Executive Board | Prior EB sanction and approved project change |

**Accountability:** The project owner coordinates the full cycle. Employees account for the money and bills in their custody. The reporting authority sanctions individual requests within its authority. The Receipt Reviewer verifies evidence. Accounts records and reconciles finances. Leadership decides funding and corrective actions. One person's review does not silently replace another required approval.

**Escalation:** If a handover is incomplete, return it with the specific correction required and a named owner. If funding, reports or evidence are overdue, notify the project owner, Accounts and designated reviewer. Record any authorised exception and its conditions; do not rely on an informal assurance that it will be corrected later.

## 14 Planning and funding templates

### 14.1 Annual activity and budget schedule

Record the Center, financial year, project, owner, program reviewer, budget version and approval reference. Use one row per activity and budget head; expand the schedule as needed.

| Activity and output | Expense head and estimate basis | Annual amount | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Activity reference and expected output | Quantity × rate and assumptions | INR | INR | INR | INR | INR |
| Total | Reconcile to approved annual budget | INR | INR | INR | INR | INR |

Add funding sources, permitted-use conditions, prior commitments and the cash-payment forecast. The quarterly totals must reconcile to the annual amount; explain costs or payments whose timing differs.

### 14.2 Quarterly allocation request

- Period, Center, project and approval reference: ____________________
- Planned activities and outputs for the quarter: ____________________
- Forecast cash payments still needed, including unpaid obligations once: INR ______
- Money already held and authorised for those payments: INR ______
- Net additional allocation requested: INR ______
- Previous allocation support: monthly report references, bill index, posted expenses, returns and closing balances.
- Open items: amount, reason, custodian, corrective action and due date.
- Accounts recommendation and reviewer: ____________________
- Fund authority decision, conditions, amount and date: ____________________
- Actual releases: advance or supplier-payment reference, date and amount.

### 14.3 Advance Purpose and supporting plan

Enter a concise Purpose in the request: **activity and date; planned items and amount breakdown; total; required-by date; expected settlement date; balance already held for the same activity.** Attach the approved activity plan or detailed estimate where useful. The Purpose replaces the generic email request to the Director.

**Purpose example:** October community program, 20 October: food materials INR 8,000 and local transport INR 2,000, total INR 10,000. Funds needed by 18 October; expected settlement 27 October. Activity OCT-01. No balance held for this activity.

The supporting plan should identify the responsible employee, project, allocation reference, funding source and any related advance or pending request. Confirm the expected settlement date with the reporting authority rather than assuming the monthly report deadline automatically extends it.

### 14.4 Budget change request

Identify the existing budget version, affected activities or heads, old and proposed amounts, reason, funding implications and effect on outputs. Obtain **Executive Board sanction before reallocating**, then submit the corresponding project change with the sanction attached. Keep both the previous and revised versions.

## 15 Monthly report template

**Header:** Center ______; project ______; month and year ______; prepared by ______; submitted on ______; reviewed by ______. Ordinarily submit by the 15th of the following month. Identify the report version and links to supporting records.

### 15.1 Activity results

| Activity reference and date | Planned and actual output | Expense and evidence links | Variance and next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One row per activity | State delivery and beneficiary or participation figures | Bill or claim references and activity evidence | Explain changes; name owner and due date |

Include postponed or cancelled activities and a brief narrative explaining the month's results. Report a month with no activity as a nil-activity report, showing any outstanding financial balances.

### 15.2 Budget and expenditure

| Budget head | Approved annual budget | Posted actual this month | Posted actual to date | Pending commitments | Available after commitments |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One row per head | INR | INR | INR | INR | INR |
| Total | INR | INR | INR | INR | INR |

Available after commitments = approved annual budget − posted actual to date − open commitments not already included in posted actual. Reconcile to Accounts and explain any additional known cost or difference from Budget Health. Show the quarter's allocation, actual releases and utilisation in a separate schedule; they are not expenditure columns.

### 15.3 Advance and payment schedules

For each advance, record: employee or custodian; advance reference; opening unsettled balance; paid this month; posted adjustments this month; actual returns this month; closing unsettled balance; pending bill amount; expected settlement date; action owner. Use the reconciliation in section 8.

List unpaid approved claims and supplier invoices separately with amount, expense period and proposed payment date. Include the month's payment and return references so Accounts can match cash movements to the bank or cash records.

### 15.4 Attachments and open items

- Activity evidence and explanations for significant variances.
- Bill and claim index showing invoice date, amount, project, activity, funding source, advance, claim status and voucher reference.
- Advance, unpaid-liability and fund-utilisation schedules.
- Relevant cash or bank evidence and Accounts reconciliation references.
- EB sanctions, allocation decisions and approved exceptions relevant to the month.
- Open-item list showing the issue, amount, owner, due date and resolution.
- Next month's activities, expected cash need and decisions requested.

**Review record:** Project owner confirms completeness; Accounts records financial checks and corrections; the designated leadership reviewer records accepted results, actions and funding implications. Corrections keep a version and date.

## 16 Worked example of a continuing cycle

**Illustrative amounts only.** A Center has an approved annual project budget of INR 1,20,000 and an October–December allocation of INR 30,000. The annual budget remains INR 1,20,000 in the project. The allocation register separately records the INR 30,000 quarterly authority.

**October planning and release.** The owner plans an activity requiring INR 10,000. The employee enters the cost breakdown in the advance Purpose field, with the project, timing and settlement plan. The request receives the required approvals and Accounts disburses INR 10,000. The remaining quarterly release authority is INR 20,000, assuming there are no other releases.

**Delivery and settlement.** Accepted bills total INR 8,000. They are verified, sanctioned, finalised and adjusted against the employee's advance. The employee returns INR 2,000 and Accounts records the return. The advance closes at zero: INR 10,000 paid − INR 8,000 adjusted − INR 2,000 returned. The project has incurred INR 8,000 of expense; it has not incurred INR 10,000 merely because that amount was advanced.

**October report.** By 15 November, the owner submits the activity report, bill index, claim and voucher references and the advance reconciliation. The financial schedule shows INR 8,000 posted expenditure, INR 10,000 gross release and INR 2,000 return as separate figures. The return does not automatically increase the approved gross release ceiling; Accounts follows the allocation decision on any re-release.

**November continuation.** Leadership reviews the activity results and Accounts checks the balances. The owner costs the next activity and requests only the money required within the available release authority. If INR 3,000 must move from transport to another approved activity or budget head, the owner obtains EB sanction before making that reallocation and records the approved project change.

**Next quarter.** The request for January–March includes the preceding quarter's bills, monthly reports, actual releases, returns and remaining balances. Accounts recommends the next release only after checking adequate support. The fund authority records its decision and the cycle continues.

## 17 What happens when a stage is incomplete

| Situation | Required response | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget or project approval missing | Obtain approval and usable project setup before spending | Project owner |
| Bill unclear or missing | Correct or document the issue; show it as pending support | Employee and Receipt Reviewer |
| Cost exceeds a head or changes activity | Seek prior EB reallocation sanction and the necessary project revision | Project owner |
| Advance has an unexplained balance | Reconcile, obtain support or actual return, and record follow-up | Employee and Accounts |
| Monthly pack incomplete at the deadline | Submit the available pack with open items and dated follow-up | Center leader |
| Previous allocation inadequately supported | Hold further release and refer for a documented corrective decision | Accounts and fund authority |
| Duplicate or wrong-period posting found | Make an authorised correction with links to the original record | Accounts |

## 18 Arrangements to confirm before rollout

The cycle is ready to operate once management confirms the people, approval assignments and supporting records below. These decisions should be recorded with the approved version of the guide.

- Name the annual-budget and quarterly-allocation authorities, including any delegation from the Executive Board. Confirm the program reviewer for non-Center projects.
- Confirm the Accounts and leadership review dates proposed for the 20th and 25th. Align the earlier accounting-policy draft's monthly close timetable with the 15-day submission deadline.
- Name the recipient and submission channel for monthly packs, the custodian of the allocation register, and the people who verify and release bank payments.
- Confirm the current approved bill standards and SF Type B monthly report version, and how the schedules in this guide accompany them.
- Set expectations for advance settlement dates, permitted retention of funds, delayed submissions and escalation. The monthly report deadline does not replace an advance's agreed settlement date.
- Confirm how quarterly release authority treats returned funds, partial releases, retained balances and re-release requests.

## 19 Records and system support

Use the project and advance workflows for their respective approvals and financial transactions. Keep the quarterly allocation decision and monthly pack as controlled supporting records, with references in the relevant project or transaction. A record kept outside the portal must still have an owner, version, approval reference and traceable link.

The quarterly fund-release check and review calendar require an operating register and follow-up by the named people. Do not assume that approving an advance automatically proves adequate support for the previous Center allocation.
